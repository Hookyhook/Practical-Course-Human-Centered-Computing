% 2. §3.4 -- annotation counts.  Tallied from the four returned forms
%    (form_spanish, form_french, form_chinese, form_arabic).
%    Item count is 155, not 160: the Arabic form omits C3_word_splitting

%    "Natural?" is a 1-5 scale on the form, not a pass/fail criterion.

Native or fluent speakers reviewed Spanish, French, Chinese and Arabic,
covering three scripts. For each language one annotator examined the
eight model-generated prompts, then judged five source posts under all
applicable perturbations for whether the edit was applied, whether the
output was natural on a five-point scale, and whether meaning was handled
as specified. This gives 155 annotated variants---40 per language except
Arabic, where word splitting was excluded because it failed to apply to
every Arabic post---with one annotator per language and no agreement
statistics, so it is a diagnostic check and not an estimate of verifier
accuracy.

Of the 150 completed application judgements, 80 items were judged to have
applied the intended transformation, 23 partially and 47 not; of 149
completed meaning judgements, 97 handled meaning as specified and 51 did
not; mean naturalness was $3.3$, with 63 items rated 4 or 5. The four
languages separate sharply (Table~\ref{tab:human}): Spanish and French
sit above the mean on all three criteria, Chinese and Arabic below it,
and Arabic is the weakest on every criterion.

% standard orthography") is NOT supported: mean character-level distance
% is 0.224 for Patois and 0.228 for AAE, and Singlish is highest (0.272)
% while being second least damaging.  Token overlap does track the

% dialects verified:
%
%   dialect    degradation(A)   new tokens %   token Jaccard
%   Patois          10.1            43.4           0.402

%   Singlish         3.7            33.1           0.483
%   AAE              2.6            33.4           0.533

The ordering is stable across both tasks and tracks how much of the
original wording each rewrite replaces rather than how far it departs
syntactically. Over the $3{,}002$ Task~A claims for which all four
dialect rewrites verify, mean token overlap with the original falls
monotonically with degradation (Jaccard $0.53$ for African American
English, $0.48$ Singlish, $0.45$ Nigerian Pidgin, $0.40$ Jamaican
Patois), while sentence-level character distance does not order the four
at all ($0.22$--$0.27$, Patois lowest). Patois rewrites replace $43\%$ of
tokens with forms absent from the original against $33\%$ for African
American English (Table~\ref{tab:dialect-examples}). Dialect degradation
is therefore in the same range as our character-level family and well
below typos, consistent with these edits acting on the models primarily
through lexical form.
